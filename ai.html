01 / INTRODUCTION

What is Artificial Intelligence?

Artificial Intelligence, commonly known as AI, refers to the ability of computer systems and machines to perform tasks that normally require human intelligence. These tasks include learning from information, recognising patterns, understanding language, solving problems, making predictions and supporting decision-making.

AI systems use algorithms, data and computational techniques to analyse information and discover useful patterns. Based on these patterns, an AI system can produce predictions, recommendations, decisions or responses.

Unlike traditional computer programs that mainly follow a fixed set of instructions, some AI systems can learn from data and improve their performance when provided with more relevant information. For example, an image recognition system can learn from labelled images before identifying similar objects in new images.

Artificial Intelligence includes several important areas such as Machine Learning, Natural Language Processing, Computer Vision and Robotics. Machine Learning allows computers to learn patterns from data, while Natural Language Processing helps machines understand and generate human language.

Today, AI is used in healthcare, education, transportation, finance, manufacturing, entertainment and many other industries. AI technologies are also present in everyday applications such as smartphones, navigation systems, recommendation systems, search engines and virtual assistants.

Simple Definition Artificial Intelligence enables machines to analyse information, learn from patterns and perform tasks that normally require human intelligence.

02 / TYPES OF AI

Types of Artificial Intelligence

Artificial Intelligence can be classified according to its level of capability. Three commonly discussed categories are Narrow AI, General AI and Super AI. Narrow AI is the type currently used in practical real-world systems.

01
CURRENTLY EXISTS

Narrow AI

Narrow AI, also known as Weak AI, is designed to perform a specific task or a limited group of related tasks.

It does not possess general human-like intelligence. Instead, it operates within the area for which it was designed.

Most AI systems used today belong to this category. They may perform a particular task very effectively but cannot automatically apply that ability to unrelated problems.

Real Examples Recommendation systems, virtual assistants, image recognition systems, navigation tools, spam filters and customer-service chatbots.
02
THEORETICAL

General AI

Artificial General Intelligence refers to a theoretical AI system capable of understanding, learning and performing a wide range of intellectual tasks.

Unlike Narrow AI, General AI would theoretically be able to apply knowledge from one situation to another and adapt to unfamiliar problems.

Its abilities would therefore be much closer to broad human intelligence than specialised AI systems used today.

Hypothetical Example A human-like AI assistant that could learn mathematics, write programs, understand different languages, plan a journey and solve completely new problems without being designed separately for each task.
Current Status General AI has not yet been achieved and remains an area of research.
03
HYPOTHETICAL

Super AI

Artificial Super Intelligence refers to a hypothetical form of AI that would exceed human intellectual capabilities in many areas.

Such a system could theoretically demonstrate extremely advanced reasoning, creativity, learning and decision-making abilities.

Super AI is mainly discussed in relation to possible future developments and the long-term impact of Artificial Intelligence.

Hypothetical Example A future AI system that could outperform the world's best human experts in science, medicine, engineering, programming, creativity and complex problem-solving.
Current Status Super AI does not currently exist and remains a hypothetical concept.

Intelligent Navigation

Machine Learning & Prediction

Streaming platforms can analyse what users watch or listen to and compare those patterns with other information to recommend content that may match individual interests.

Real-World Example Movie, video and music recommendations used by streaming platforms
Recommendation Systems

Natural Language Processing

Real-World Example AI-assisted analysis of X-rays, CT scans and other medical images
Computer Vision
FINANCE

Financial organisations can use machine learning to analyse transaction patterns and identify unusual activities that may require additional investigation.

Real-World Example Automated monitoring of suspicious banking and card transactions

Real-World Example Smartphone facial authentication
Computer Vision

05 / BENEFITS OF AI

Benefits of Artificial Intelligence

Artificial Intelligence can provide several advantages when it is designed and used appropriately. It can assist people, improve productivity and provide useful information for decision-making.

01

Automation

AI can automate repetitive and time-consuming tasks. This allows people to focus more attention on activities that require creativity, communication and human judgement.

02

Increased Efficiency

AI systems can process large amounts of information quickly, helping certain tasks to be completed more efficiently.

03

Data Analysis

AI can identify useful patterns inside large datasets that may be difficult to discover manually. These patterns can support better-informed decisions.

04

Improved Accuracy

When properly designed and evaluated, AI can provide consistent results and help reduce some forms of repetitive human error.

05

Continuous Availability

Automated AI systems can provide services continuously. For example, automated customer support can answer basic questions outside normal office hours.

06

Personalisation

AI can analyse preferences and behaviour to provide personalised recommendations, learning materials and digital experiences.

06 / CHALLENGES OF AI

Challenges of Artificial Intelligence

Although AI provides many useful capabilities, the technology also introduces technical, ethical and social challenges. Responsible development requires these issues to be considered carefully.

Privacy & Data Security

AI systems may process large amounts of personal information. Suitable privacy controls and security measures are therefore important for protecting users.

Bias & Fairness

AI systems can produce unfair or inaccurate results when their training data contains bias or does not adequately represent different users and situations.

Changes to Employment

Automation may change responsibilities in some occupations and reduce the need for certain repetitive tasks while increasing demand for new digital skills.

Transparency

Some AI systems can be difficult to interpret. Understanding why a system produced a particular result may be important, especially for significant decisions.

Security & Misuse

AI technology can be misused to support harmful activities or create misleading information. Appropriate safeguards and responsible use are therefore important.

Human Responsibility

AI should not automatically be assumed to be correct. Human judgement and supervision remain important, particularly when decisions may significantly affect people.
